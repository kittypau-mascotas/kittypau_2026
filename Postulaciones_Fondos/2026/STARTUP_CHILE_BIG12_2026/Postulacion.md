9. What phase of development is your startup in? *
Concept or idea: You have an initial idea and some hypotheses about the problem you're trying to solve. There is no prototype, no users or customers, and no in-depth research into the problem or solution.

Preliminary validation and research: You have an initial study and hypotheses about the problem you're trying to solve. There is research, interviews, and early ideas regarding potential solutions to the problem. There is no prototype, no users or customers.

Initial prototype (pre MVP): You have a low-resolution prototype, pre MVP, that allows you to test initial hypotheses, but it is not yet commercially viable. It has been tested with a few users, friends, or family members in a safe, controlled environment, gathering feedback, suggestions, and identifying areas for improvement. There are no sales yet.

MVP: You have a functional MVP, with no sales. It is ready to be presented to potential clients and tested under real-market conditions. You may have a small number of users, downloads, usage tests, or intention to pilot, which provides initial metrics on users or potential clients.

MVP with clients: You have a functional MVP, and some sporadic, non-recurring initial sales. You have some users, downloads, trials, or pilot projects, which provide usage and satisfaction metrics. You have a general understanding of your potential clients, but there are many assumptions that still need to be validated. You are iterating on the prototype based on feedback and insights gathered from the market.

Recurring clients: You have your first recurring sales and initial signs of product-market fit. The potential business niche and clients profile have been identified, so you're working on developments and/or modifications that, hypothetically, will increase sales in that segment, but this will soon be validated.

Initial expansion phase: You have product-market fit, recurring and retained clients, initial contracts, and a clearly defined market niche. You have an initial expansion strategy that is being tested and optimized, but it still has assumptions that need to be validated. Operational costs are covered by revenue on a sporadic or seasonal basis.

Scaling sales: You have product-market fit, standardized products packaged by segment, and scalable offerings. You have stable contracts, highly loyal clients, and a steady revenue stream that covers operational costs. The product or solution is already well-established in the market, and you have a deep understanding of the customer, as well as the barriers to entry and exit. There is a clear growth plan in place, and the goal is to expand into additional regions or countries.

International Scale-Up (USD 1M+): You have high market demand, large clients, and contracts. Annual sales exceed USD 1 million, and you have a stable presence in two or more countries.﻿
